This isn't a perfectly fair comparison – our label set is harder. The published benchmark uses 5 classes: angry, happy, sad, neutral, excited. Ours adds a frustrated-vs-upset distinction, separated only by degree of anger, which other published work sidesteps by folding frustration into an “others” bucket. Our weakest class is frustrated (F1 0.44 this run, as low as 0.33 in other configs), and that's the exact boundary we added on top of the published task.

Where the ceiling actually is

  • Fine-tuned multimodal SOTA on IEMOCAP 4-class reaches about 76.3% weighted accuracy (GatedxLSTM); fine-tuned wav2vec2/HuBERT reaches about 73%. On real call-center audio, the same class of model has been reported to drop to 55–65%, which is the actual deployment target here.
  • IEMOCAP's own inter-annotator agreement is only fair: Fleiss’ κ 0.27 across all classes, 0.48 on the four main ones. That caps what any system can score, including the human labelers who built the benchmark.
  • Closing the gap to fine-tuned SOTA needs thousands of labeled emotional utterances. We were given three. We chose prompt-based classification over transcript and acoustic features because it's competitive without that scale of data – see the two-approach comparison in TECHNICAL_MEMO.md.

Limitations

  • The IEMOCAP gains didn't visibly show up on the 3 real clips we were given. Tone stayed at 1/3 correct across all 8 configurations we tested, with and without diarization, at 4 effort levels. With only 3 clips, each one is worth 33 percentage points, so this sample can't confirm the +0.06 macro-F1 gain we measured from adding context, and it can't rule it out either.
  • frustrated vs upset is the weakest boundary, distinguished only by degree of anger. Published literature finds this axis hardest too, and other systems typically avoid it by folding frustration into an “other” bucket instead of reporting it separately.
  • The 7 non-LLM fields are validated on synthetic degradations, at a scale (n=20–65) too small for a production sign-off. Real audio often has multiple problems in the same clip, and a synthetic generator that applies one degradation at a time won't reproduce that.
  • confidence is an uncalibrated placeholder. All 3 labeled examples we were given share the same constant (0.82), so there's no ground truth to calibrate against yet. This is a known, documented gap.

Full methodology (leave-one-out and grouped validation, the two approaches compared, all ablations and seed-replication runs) is in TECHNICAL_MEMO.md and PLAN.md in the repository root.
